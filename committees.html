        Who Does What

        You lead the conversation. Staff handles the logistics. The Board sets direction.

        Board of Directors
        Establishes priorities and provides direction and approval where required.
        Committee Chair
        Leads the committee, helps advance its priorities, facilitates discussion, monitors progress on tasks, and represents the committee to the Board.
        Vice-Chair
        Supports the chair and runs meetings when the chair is unavailable.
        Staff Liaison
        Coordinates logistics, prepares meeting materials and minutes, and connects the committee to CLEAR staff.

        Changing over in September. New chairs take over at the close of the Annual Educational Conference. Staff will still check in with outgoing chairs about minutes and immediate follow-up from the September meeting.

        Your Priorities: Statements of Direction

        Each year the Board gives your committee two or three new priorities, on top of routine work like environmental scans and programming topic development. Here's how they move through the year.

        1. The Board Writes ThemStatements of direction draw on the strategic plan and on ongoing and new tasks.
        2. You Receive Them at the Start of the Committee YearStaff shares them with your committee in the fall.
        3. Your Committee Reviews ThemRaise concerns or ask for clarification before the Midyear Business Meeting.
        4. They Shape Your AgendasThe tasks become at least part of the agenda at your meetings from then on.
        5. You Report Your Plans and ProgressDescribe your plan for each task and how it's going in your Midyear and Annual Conference reports to the Board.

        Need a smaller team? You can set up a temporary working group for a specific project, like reviewing curriculum, writing a publication, or building a resource. The chair or another volunteer can lead it. Working groups meet by videoconference as needed and report back to the full committee on a schedule the committee sets.

        Before the Meeting

        Every agenda includes a review of minutes and a quick look at the charter and statements of direction, to keep discussion focused.

        • ChairShare your availability and how often you'd like to meet.
        • StaffPoll members to find a date that works for most, then send the invitations.
        • StaffDraft the agenda 1–2 weeks before the meeting.
        • ChairReview the draft agenda with the vice-chair, add items for ongoing projects, and approve it.

        During the Meeting

        If the chair can't attend, the vice-chair leads.

        • ChairOpen the meeting and set an inclusive tone.
        • ChairGuide the conversation so every agenda item is covered, and invite input from all members.
        • ChairSummarize decisions and next steps, and confirm who owns each follow-up and when it's due.
        • StaffHost the Zoom meeting, share the agenda on screen, and take minutes.
        • StaffAnswer questions from the chair and members.

        After the Meeting

        • StaffCompile the minutes and send them to the chair.
        • ChairReview and approve the minutes.
        • StaffSend the minutes to the committee with reminders about assignments.
        • ChairTrack project progress, follow up with members who have assignments, and update staff. Your liaison can help.

        Making Decisions

        Committees decide together. Aim for consensus first, and vote only when you need to. You don't need a formal motion or a second.

        When to Involve the Board

        Your committee can act on anything that fits its charter and approved priorities. Bring a recommendation to the Board first when it has significant financial impact or would be a major change to a CLEAR program, service, or publication.

          Reporting to the Board

          Twice a year you'll tell the Board what your committee decided and where it stands on its priorities. It's a short bullet list, not meeting minutes.

          Information Items

          Updates on progress toward your statement of direction goals.

          Action Items

          Proposed actions that need Board review and approval before you proceed.

          1. Your Committee Meets in January or SeptemberBoard reports are part of these two meetings.
          2. You and Staff Build the ReportYour liaison works with you on the bullet list after the meeting.
          3. You Present It to the BoardYou, or someone you designate, give the report at the Board meeting. In person is preferred, and a virtual option may be available.
          4. You Share the Board's ResponseYou and your staff liaison share the Board's feedback or decision at the committee's next meeting.

          Don't wait if something comes up. Your staff liaison can send a recommendation to CLEAR's executive director at any time for the next available Board meeting agenda.
